Five systems. One scaling objective.

More institutional work.
Not more overhead.

Macchi & Macchi already operates where boutique firms are hardest to scale: large real-estate and development projects, local permitting, complex institutional relationships and matters that can run for months or years. The objective is not to automate lawyering. It is to remove the repeatable preparation, context rebuilding and coordination that forces growth to become a hiring problem.

The test is simple: can the current attorneys absorb materially more client work while spending a larger percentage of their day on hearings, court, negotiation, strategy and counsel?

Scale before hiring

The goal is not to replace lawyers. It is to make capacity—not internal workload—the last reason to hire one.

New attorneys should be added when client coverage, hearings, court appearances, geography, conflicts or scheduling demand another human. The system should absorb as much repeatable internal work as possible before payroll has to grow.

Macchi Standard of Practice

The system should make the firm’s best way of working repeatable.

It cannot guarantee a win, and it should never substitute for legal judgment. What it can do is prevent a matter from being treated as “ready” when required work, evidence, approvals or review steps are still incomplete.

Approved knowledgeOnly permissioned precedent, playbooks and source-linked matter history feed the workflow.
Mandatory quality gatesRequired tasks must be completed or explicitly waived by an authorized attorney before status moves forward.
Escalation logicNovel, high-risk or out-of-standard issues are automatically routed to the appropriate senior attorney.
Learning loopApproved corrections and outcomes improve future checklists and retrieval without silently rewriting firm standards.

Commercial model

AI efficiency should improve economics—not erase revenue.

The answer is not to bill hours that were never worked. For repeatable, well-defined work, the opportunity is to use historical itemized billing data to create reasonable fixed-task or hybrid fees that share efficiency with the client while increasing revenue per attorney hour.

Historical time becomes a pricing benchmark—not fictional time on an invoice.
01Mine historical billsFind recurring tasks and actual time distributions.
02Create complexity bandsStandard / Complex / Exceptional.
03Set defined task feeClient gets predictability and a share of efficiency.
04RebenchmarkActual touch time and outcomes continuously improve pricing.

Defined-service economics

Historical time establishes what the work has required. AI changes what the work will require. The difference becomes economic leverage.

01 · MINE HISTORICAL MATTERSAnalyze prior invoices, time entries, matter types and transaction histories: attorney hours, paralegal hours, support effort, variance, complexity, exceptions, delivery cost and client revenue.
02 · CREATE COMPLEXITY BANDSSTANDARD predictable facts and workflow · COMPLEX known additional variables · EXCEPTIONAL novel issues or significant attorney judgment.
03 · USE HISTORY AS THE BENCHMARKHistorical time becomes empirical evidence for an agreed fixed, defined-service or hybrid fee. It does not become fictional time on a future invoice.
04 · APPLY AI TO DELIVERY COSTLess attorney preparation + less paralegal processing + less administrative coordination = lower actual delivery cost.
AGREED CLIENT FEE − ACTUAL AI-ENABLED DELIVERY COST = CONTRIBUTION MARGINHourly matters remain actual-time billing. Historical time can inform an agreed fixed or defined-service fee; it is never used to manufacture hours on an hourly invoice.

The client buys the defined legal service. Macchi retains the value of delivering it more intelligently.
